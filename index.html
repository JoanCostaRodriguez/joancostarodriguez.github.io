Conflict, Persistence, and Route Switching: Migration Corridor Allocation Under Changing Transit Risk

Research Proposal · Barcelona School of Economics · April 2026

This paper studies how conflict along transit corridors reshapes the allocation of East African irregular migration flows toward Europe. Using geocoded UCDP conflict events overlaid on the road network, I construct a continuous, time-varying measure of corridor traversal cost and estimate the persistence-reallocation trade-off across the Eastern and Central Mediterranean routes. The identification exploits three major conflict escalations: the Tigray War (2020), the Sudan civil war (2023) and the Gaza conflict (2023), which differentially affect the two corridors. Preliminary results show strong corridor persistence and a 16 percentage-point reallocation of route shares following the Sudan war onset.

Remittance Frictions, Migrant Heterogeneity, and Transfer Behavior

Research Proposal · Barcelona School of Economics · April 2026

This paper adapts the heterogeneous-firm trade framework to migrant remittance decisions. Migrants differ in altruism and income, remitting requires paying fixed and variable transfer costs, and only those with sufficiently high net benefit participate. Using SIPP micro data (2019–2024) and World Bank transfer-cost data, I estimate the extensive and intensive margins of remitting and recover an elasticity of substitution close to unity. The framework yields testable predictions on recipient-type selection and enables counterfactuals for cost-reduction and digitalization policies.

Market Access and Regional Inequality: Evidence from Brazil's Capital Relocation

Awarded Undergraduate Thesis · Pompeu Fabra University

This paper examines the impact of market access on regional inequality using the relocation of Brazil's capital to Brasília as a natural experiment. The capital move triggered a large-scale highway expansion that differentially reduced trade costs across states. I construct market access indices from official data and find that increases in market access are associated with rising inequality in industrial regions, while the effect is significantly attenuated in rural areas.

Ideology and Age: A Perspective Across Multiple Generations in Spain, the United Kingdom, and the United States

Undergraduate Paper · Pompeu Fabra University

This study examines political trends across generations in Spain, comparing them with the United Kingdom and the United States. Using CIS survey data, the research documents an initial leftward lean in younger generations that shifts toward more conservative positions with age, shaped by historical events, economic circumstances, and institutional context.
